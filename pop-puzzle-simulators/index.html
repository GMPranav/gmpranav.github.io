Prince of Persia: The Forgotten Sands · TFS_WaterPillar3.CPP

Water Pillar 3 Puzzle Simulator

The Water Pillar puzzle features three stacked rotating tiers (Lower, Middle and Upper), each sitting at position 1–4. A Normal lever move turns every piece. A Freeze move holds back any piece that is at position 4 (clockwise) or position 1 (anti-clockwise); the Upper and Middle tiers each hold back together with a hidden joint piece. The puzzle is solved when all three tiers are at position 4 (the game gives no indication).

Optimization Insights

Speedrun Bruteforce Architecture

STATE SPACE MAPPING

Why Bruteforce?

In speedruns, solving puzzles by intuition or standard intended player routes wastes dozens of seconds. Because each move has a deterministic transition matrix, we can model the entire game puzzle as a directed graph and compute the Breadth-First Search (BFS) shortest path to discover the fastest possible inputs.

T2T STATUE CONSTRAINTS

The Two Thrones Matrix

The King's Statue puzzle uses a 2D transition table indexed by [rotation][translation]. Certain tiles block forward motion if the statue is facing the walls or dead ends, and the puzzle is only won by moving forward from step 4 while facing Front. The C++ bruteforcer searches these states and finds a shortest solution of 13 moves.

TFS PERMUTATIONS

Differential Gear Rings

The Forgotten Sands puzzles involve coupled modulo arithmetic (e.g. turn(piece, d) = (piece - 1 + d + 4) % 4 + 1) and rings that share slots. The bruteforcer runs a breadth-first search that merges identical states, so searching up to 15 moves stays fast even for the Wii gems puzzle (about 7.3 million states).
